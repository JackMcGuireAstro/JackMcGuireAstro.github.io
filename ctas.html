Cowboy Transient Alert System

CTAS

CTAS brings public supernova and multimessenger alerts together with follow-up reports and information already known about the same area of sky. It gives researchers one public place to see what was reported, what others have found, and which candidates may deserve a closer look.

Active development Version 1.2.0

CTAS in brief

A transient is CTAS’s operational umbrella for a public, time-dependent astronomical event or candidate that may warrant follow-up. It can be optical, gamma-ray, X-ray, radio, neutrino, gravitational-wave or another multimessenger alert. Inclusion does not confirm a discovery, classification, host or counterpart.

The CTAS score is a 0–100 follow-up ordering aid, not a probability or a measure of scientific importance. Higher scores generally identify recent, bright or information-poor candidates that may benefit from attention sooner.

How the score is calculated

Start at 35; add up to 24 for recency, −8 to +20 for reported discovery brightness, +12 if unclassified, +8 for conflicting classifications, +7 when no spectrum is retained, and up to +10 when the latest observation is old. Subtract 1.5 per retained quantitative observation, up to 12. Add +4 per distinct messenger channel, up to 20. Retracted or bogus records score zero; the final value is clipped to 0–100.

What CTAS is

A public research catalog that collects rights-cleared alerts and follow-up records, preserves source provenance, and supports researcher-led triage and analysis.

What CTAS does not do

CTAS does not control telescopes, submit observing requests, publish classifications or make discovery claims. Restricted or uncertain-rights content is not included in the public export.

How a record moves through CTAS

Public intake → rights screening and normalization → context and triage → evidence-aware analysis → follow-up drafting → sanitized public export. Conflicts, retractions and insufficient-data states remain visible.

How it is built

Local Python services, scientific libraries and SQLite ingest and analyze records. A sanitized static export is checked about every two minutes and published to this GitHub Pages site; credentials and internal records are excluded.

More about CTAS

CTAS is active research software designed and developed by Jack McGuire. Scientists decide which source-attributed records and derived products are appropriate for their own work. Ask Jack about CTAS.
