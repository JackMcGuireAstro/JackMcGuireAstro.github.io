Run sheet

CTAS transients for one night at one site, ready to print: coordinates, tonight’s window and airmass curve, a finder chart and the S/N-20 exposure at WIRO and Red Buttes. A CTAS dossier opens this page with its own target; without a list the sheet takes the ten follow-up targets up longest tonight. Everything is computed in your browser.

Standard stars

Standard stars are not listed yet: the Landolt (1992, 2009) and Smith et al. (2002) standard-star catalogues are not part of this site’s release, and nothing is filled in from memory. Pick a field near your targets’ airmass from Landolt (2009), J/AJ/137/4186, Landolt (1992), II/183A or, for u′g′r′i′z′, Smith et al. (2002), J/AJ/123/2121.

How this is worked out

The window and airmass come from the same almanac formulae as the Tonight planner (dark means the Sun below −12°). The exposure is the CTAS suggested follow-up’s S/N-20 estimate in r at the target’s best airmass tonight, from the exposure calculator’s instrument profiles; values still marked placeholder there are marked here too. The brightness is the latest magnitude the CTAS release retained, not a forecast. The finder chart is a plain image from CDS hips2fits; if it does not load, the coordinates and the full finder chart link still work.
