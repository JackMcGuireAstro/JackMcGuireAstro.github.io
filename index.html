01 / Research

Reading between
the spectral lines.

A star’s surface can hold the chemical record of a companion’s past. I use that record to investigate how binary systems evolve.

Manuscript in preparation

Stellar chemistry &
binary mass transfer

Barium dwarfs carry heavy elements inherited from a companion that has since become a white dwarf. Their chemistry offers a way to trace an interaction long after it happened.

My work connects abundance measurements, companion evidence, and a literature census to investigate which systems we find, which we miss, and what that tells us about mass transfer.

  • Spectroscopy
  • Gaia astrometry
  • Radial velocities
  • Binary evolution

Contribution, methods & earlier work

I led the sample analysis with Dr. Russel J. White at Georgia State University, first as a Raghavan Fellow and then as an undergraduate research assistant. I am first author on the manuscript in preparation.

The earlier analysis combined Gaia DR3, archival spectroscopy, radial-velocity statistics, spectral-energy-distribution fitting with VOSA, and MIST models. The manuscript is in preparation; it is not listed here as a submitted or accepted paper.

The 40 Eridani investigation

Working with Dr. Richard O. Gray, I searched for s-process enrichment in the 40 Eridani system. The reported search found no detectable enrichment. That is a constraint on the search, not proof that mass transfer never occurred; this investigation has not been published separately.

Cosmic rays, closer to home.

Photometry / Published 2025

Four nights with 797 Montana.

Our observing campaign measured the main-belt asteroid’s rotation period at 4.545 ± 0.001 hours, using the 24-inch Miller telescope at Hard Labor Creek Observatory.

A little about

From Georgia to Wyoming.

I started my Ph.D. at the University of Wyoming in August 2026. Alongside research and coursework, I’m a graduate teaching assistant in the Department of Physics & Astronomy.

I came here from Georgia State University, where I earned an Associate of Science in Physics with High Honors in 2024 and a Bachelor of Science in Physics in May 2026. A summer as a Raghavan Fellow with Dr. Russel J. White helped turn my interest in stellar chemistry into a research direction.

Away from research, I play guitar and bass, cook more ambitiously than is strictly reasonable, and travel whenever the calendar allows it. Buckeye believes she is a person.
