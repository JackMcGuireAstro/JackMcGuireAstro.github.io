Finder charts

A finder chart is a picture of the sky around your target, so you can recognise the field at the telescope. Type an object name or its coordinates, and choose how much sky to show and which survey to use. The chart marks the target and shows which way is north and east, with a scale bar. It prints on one page. You can also enter a nearby bright “offset star” to get the move from that star to the target.

The survey image comes from the CDS hips2fits service. The page sends it only the position, field size and survey. If you type a name, the name is sent to the CDS Sesame name resolver. Nothing else is sent, and nothing is stored.

How this is worked out

The image is cut from the survey’s HiPS tiles by CDS hips2fits in a gnomonic (TAN) projection on ICRS axes. It is centred on your target and 1000 pixels across, so north is up and east is left, as on the sky seen from inside the celestial sphere. DSS2 and 2MASS cover the whole sky. Pan-STARRS covers only declinations north of −30°. The plate scale is the field of view divided by 1000 pixels: 0.6″ per pixel for a 10′ field.

The overlay is drawn by this page on top of the image, so it does not depend on the image loading. The reticle leaves the target itself clear. The scale bar is a round length no longer than a quarter of the field. Survey images are years or decades old. A transient or a high proper-motion star will not be at the marked position in the image, so check the epoch when it matters.

The offset is measured on the tangent plane centred on the offset star. East and north are arcseconds on the sky: ΔRA·cos δ and ΔDec. It is exact at any distance, which matters near the celestial poles, where the small-angle shortcut fails. The move is what the telescope must do after you centre the offset star. Many control systems define the sign of an offset the other way round, so check yours.

Printing keeps just the chart and its caption on one A4 or Letter page. The image is shown in black on white (inverted), and the overlay is drawn in black.
